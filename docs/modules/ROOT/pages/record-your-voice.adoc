= Record your voice
:repo: https://github.com/ZirekHQ/voice-models
:script: {repo}/tree/main/data/recording-script
:script-file: {repo}/blob/main/data/recording-script

Text-to-speech models for any language need voice recordings from native speakers.
Kurmanji comes first because that is where the project started, but Sorani, Zazaki and every other language, Kurdish or not, are welcome.
The voices are used by blind people with NVDA, often at two to three times normal speed, so every sound has to stay clear.
Consistency matters more than expressiveness.

Anyone who speaks the language natively can volunteer.
To start, open an issue on the {repo}/issues[voice-models repository] saying which language you speak.
GitHub issues are public, so post only your language, the consent line and your credit choice, never contact details or recordings; the consent does not cover what you write in an issue.
If you prefer not to use a public issue, contact a maintainer through the https://github.com/ZirekHQ[organization page].
We reply with access to the upload folder and help setting up your recording app.
Start with batch 1: it is useful on its own, and you can continue from there.

The guide below applies to every language.
Details that belong to one language, such as its script and pronunciation notes, are under <<language-notes,Language notes>>.
For a language that has no script yet, we prepare one with you.

== Recording terms

Read the xref:recording-consent.adoc[recording consent] before you record.
In short: your recordings are shared for non-commercial use, a business can use them only under a written agreement with Zirek, and a published voice cannot be recalled.
Confirm your agreement in your opening issue with the line "I agree to the recording consent v1.0", and say whether the voice should credit you by name, by pseudonym or not at all.

== What you read

Each language has a script: a list of sentences split into batches of about one hour of finished audio each.

* Each line has an id. Save each recording as `<id>.wav`.
* Every speaker of a language reads the same sentences in the same order.
* The first batch covers the common sound pairs of the language, so it alone is enough to train a test voice.
* The core set is about 10 hours per speaker. We train on it first and decide afterwards whether more hours are worth recording. More than 10 to 20 hours per speaker barely improves a Piper voice, and long sessions make voice and pace drift, which hurts clarity.
* If a sentence is wrong in your language, has a typo, or is something you would not say, skip it and add its id to a `skipped.txt` file. Do not fix it while reading.

== Room and equipment

* Use a small, quiet room with soft furnishings such as curtains, a sofa or a bed. Turn off fans, fridges and heating noise, and silence your phone.
* Use the same microphone, in the same position, in the same room, for every session. A USB condenser microphone with a pop filter is fine.
* Keep the microphone 15 to 20 cm from your mouth, slightly to the side.
* Record 48 kHz, 24-bit or 16-bit, mono WAV.
* Set the input level so normal speech peaks around -12 to -6 dBFS and never clips.
* Apply no processing: no noise reduction, compression, EQ, normalisation or reverb, in the app or afterwards. We clean up in one place.

== How to read

* Read in a calm, neutral, informative voice, like a news reader or audiobook narrator. Don't act or add emotion, even for exclamations.
* Keep a steady, moderate pace. Don't speed up as the session goes on.
* Say every consonant fully, especially at the ends of words. Don't let sentence endings fade into a whisper.
* Use your natural native pronunciation, including every sound distinction your language makes.
* Leave about half a second of silence before and after each sentence.
* If you stumble, re-record the whole sentence. Don't correct yourself mid-sentence.
* Read names, abbreviations and foreign words the way a news reader in your language would.

== Sessions

* Record at most 1 to 1.5 hours of actual reading per day, in sessions of 30 to 45 minutes with breaks. A tired voice changes how you sound, and that hurts the model.
* Record at the same time of day when you can. Keep water nearby, and avoid dairy and caffeine just before recording.
* Start every session by reading the reference sentence for your language, which is listed under <<language-notes,Language notes>> or sent with your script. Save it as `ref_YYYY-MM-DD_N.wav`, where N counts that day's sessions (`_1`, `_2`), so two sessions on one day never overwrite each other. We use it to check that your voice and setup stay the same between sessions.
* After batch 1, upload it and wait for feedback before continuing. Setup problems are cheap to fix at that point.

== Recording app

* Piper Recording Studio is the easiest option. It shows one sentence at a time, records it and saves it under its id. We load the batch files into it for you. Whichever app you use, upload mono WAV files in the format above. Check what your app saves, and tell us if it saves anything else; we check the format on your batch 1 before you continue.
* Audacity also works if you export one file per sentence, named by id.

== Handing in recordings

Upload each batch to the shared Drive folder `ziman/recordings/<speaker name>/batch_XX/`, together with that batch's `skipped.txt` and your session reference files.

== Language notes

=== Kurmanji

The Kurmanji script is public, in {script}[`data/recording-script/`] in this repository.

* It has 12,944 sentences in 13 batches. Each batch file, such as {script-file}/batches/batch_01.txt[`batch_01.txt`], has one sentence per line: the id, a tab, then the text. {script-file}/kurmanji_recording_script.csv[`kurmanji_recording_script.csv`] holds every sentence in one file.
* Batches 1 to 10 are the core, about 10 hours per speaker. Batches 11 to 13 are optional extensions.
* Batch 1 covers every common Kurmanji sound pair.
* Ids look like `kmr_00042`, so you save that recording as `kmr_00042.wav`.
* The sentences come from https://commonvoice.mozilla.org/[Common Voice] (CC0) and an earlier Kurmanji script.
* Reference sentence: _Yek, du, sê, çar, pênc, şeş, heft, heşt, neh, deh._
* Keep the difference between aspirated and plain p, t, k and ç (for example _çadir_ and _çav_), and h/ḧ and x/ẍ where you make them.
* Read names, abbreviations and foreign words the way a Kurmanji news reader would.

Notes for other languages are added here as their scripts are prepared.

== What happens next

Your recordings go into a dataset manifest under `data/manifests/`, which maps each sentence id to its audio.
The voices are trained with the recipes under `tts/` and scored on the shared benchmark in `eval/` for latency and for clarity at fast speech rates.
Published voices are listed on the xref:index.adoc#current-models[overview].
